# Chicago model: sources and reproducibility

The detailed downtown core uses public geographic data. The outer skyline, materials, street furniture and animated activity are illustrative. This is an architectural visualization, not a survey or live transit map.

## Geographic sources

- **Cook County GIS, 2022 building footprints and heights:** [feature layer](https://gis.cookcountyil.gov/traditional/rest/services/buildingFootprint_2022/MapServer/0), [metadata](https://www.arcgis.com/sharing/rest/content/items/7c4f47a3b32944e58c5f18652880fc05/info/metadata/metadata.xml?format=default&output=html). Based on 2022 lidar and imagery. The `Height` field is the highest building point above ground in feet; it can include antennas and equipment. The importer converts feet to metres. Named landmark models use curated architectural heights instead.
- **City of Chicago street centerlines:** [dataset pr57-gg9e](https://data.cityofchicago.org/d/pr57-gg9e), 2016 snapshot. The importer retains coordinates, class, direction, elevation level and node IDs. The rendered traffic network excludes lower streets, ramps and expressways. It is intentionally simplified.
- **City of Chicago water polygons:** [dataset knfe-65pw](https://data.cityofchicago.org/d/knfe-65pw), 2014 snapshot. Supplies the downtown river and lake edge.

Required City attribution: This site provides applications using data that has been modified for use from its original source, www.cityofchicago.org, the official website of the City of Chicago.

`node tools/import-city.mjs` regenerates `assets/downtown.json`. It caches downloaded source responses under ignored `.cache/`, keyed by the complete query URL including bounds and pagination. Overlapping queries deduplicate building and street source IDs. The shipped asset contains 3,678 footprint features and 2,828 street segments before runtime filtering and landmark replacements; it is about 1.3 MB uncompressed. Browser clients fetch this bundled asset once and do not contact GIS APIs.

The original source envelope is longitude −87.650 to −87.598, latitude 41.8654 to 41.9035. A second query covers the Fulton Market extension with 80 m padding. `js/coverage.js` defines the measured area shared by importing, buildings, roads, procedural exclusion and the map: downtown x −1800 to 2400 / z −2300 to 1800, plus Fulton Market x −2550 to −1750 / z −1250 to −150. Local projection uses origin −87.62786, 41.88202, with 82,950 metres per degree longitude and 110,574 metres per degree latitude. Footprints are simplified to 0.7 m tolerance. Very small features are discarded; missing heights use an explicitly flagged 12 m estimate. Materials are assigned stylistically rather than surveyed. One Prudential Plaza's main mass is corrected to 183.2 m so its antenna is not rendered as an oversized building.

## Summer waterfront

`node tools/build-waterfront.mjs` produces the bundled `assets/waterfront.json` from the existing geographic snapshot and authored landscape outlines. It runs offline using vendored **polygon-clipping 0.15.7 (MIT)**; that library is a build tool and is not loaded by visitors. The approximately 204 KB asset includes its projection, source URLs, accuracy notes, land polygons, landscape surfaces, paths, architectural anchors, source replacement IDs, trees, docks and representative moored boats.

Within x 200–3000 / z −2600–1610, land is the polygon difference of the coverage envelope and the complete City water geometry, including holes and separate rings. This replaces the old westernmost-intersection shoreline approximation and restores the Jardine peninsula without closing the harbors. The old terrain park rectangles and generic Navy Pier sheds are removed. The rest of the city retains its existing terrain. Museum Campus is outside the landscape pass.

- [Grant Park, Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/grant-ulysses-park) and the [Grant Park Lakefront Trail aerial](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/media/26906): formal fountain gardens and axes, Butler and Hutchinson fields, Petrillo setting, rail corridor, separated paths and Monroe/DuSable harbors.
- [Maggie Daley official park map](https://www.maggiedaleypark.com/assets/doc/MDP_ParkMap_1123-7705da9d0d.pdf): northern ribbon and climbing rocks, central rolling lawn, southeast play garden, tennis courts and Cancer Survivors’ Garden.
- [Navy Pier 2026 map](https://navypier.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/navy-pier-map-2026.png) and [Field Operations project photographs](https://www.fieldoperations.net/project/navy-pier): Family Pavilion, Shakespeare/The Yard, Festival Hall, Sable, Grand Ballroom, Pier Park, Wave Wall, planted South Dock, Polk Bros Park and north marina. Existing source footprints are replaced by explicit source IDs. The wheel remains 196 ft tall with 42 gondolas.
- [Oak Street to Ohio Street trail aerial](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/media/26861): beach orientation, Olive Park’s circular pools and tree-lined promenade, and the separate walking/cycling routes.

Shoreline and source footprints retain measured geometry. Landscape contours, building subdivisions and roof forms, planting, street furniture, dock placement and heights are **visual estimates**, generally within about 5–20 m in plan, rather than as-built survey models. The model illustrates built features on a typical green summer day, not temporary events, seasonal operating status, future redevelopment or actual boat/person occupancy. Reference maps and photographs are not redistributed as site assets.

The active pipeline shares the asset across terrain, procedural exclusions, route registration and the orientation map. Independent builders append named routes through `registerRoute`; they cannot silently overwrite one another. City life retains 480 vehicles, four six-car trains and eight moving riverboats. Its 800 people are split into 720 walkers and 80 cyclists. Static moored boats are instanced; no dynamic lights, particles or shadow casters are added. Existing office cards and the 18-stop tour remain unchanged.

Validation: `node tools/check-waterfront.mjs` samples the new routes every 2 m against the land and building polygons, checks representative harbor openings/peninsula support, checks trees and moored boats, verifies route-registry behavior and confirms the published data matches the source. `/tools/review.html` adds overhead/close reference views, a runtime waterfront audit and fixed baseline camera options. For comparisons, archive the previous release's `docs/` under `.cache/waterfront-baseline/`, then use the same benchmark view, lighting, desktop size and quality tier for each build.

## CTA rail — October 29, 2026 snapshot

The main **See CTA in the city** button flies directly to Morgan’s elevated platforms. The compact city card selects any modeled station, and map actions appear above the regional map. `#cta` opens Morgan directly; `#cta/<stationId>` opens a modeled station using its stable CTA ID. These navigation controls retain the original rail geometry, simulation and 18-stop architectural tour.

The CTA addition imports all eight lines, 23 active geographic service patterns, 144 station complexes including closed State/Lake, and 25 complexes within the existing downtown/Fulton Market coverage. CTA's public station totals can count the elevated and subway facilities at Clark/Lake and Roosevelt separately. Our IDs group those shared complexes but keep their platform IDs and elevations distinct.

`node tools/import-transit.mjs` generates `assets/transit.json` (about 0.81 MB). The approximately 69 MB raw GTFS ZIP stays offline in `.cache/transit/2026-10-29/`. `tools/transit-sources.lock.json` pins all five source SHA-256 hashes. Cached files are verified; a newer download with different bytes is rejected. Preserve the dated cache when moving this project: public servers do not promise indefinite access to historical feed versions.

- [CTA GTFS](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/gtfs/): route colors, geographic service shapes, station/platform IDs, ordered stops, active service on the snapshot date, and transfer records. This feed was last modified September 14, 2026. Stop times establish sequence only; animation does not replay the timetable.
- [Chicago rail GIS](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-L-Rail-Lines/xbyr-jnvx): shared physical corridor centerlines, described as November 2023 conditions and modified July 2024. The coarse surface/subway distinction does not resolve track separation or rail elevations. Red/Blue are never rendered as elevated rail inside this city's boundary, including the coarsely classified UIC–Clinton segment.
- [Station GIS](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-L-Rail-Stations/3tzw-cg4m) and [directional stops](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-System-Information-List-of-L-Stops/8pix-ypme): independent station crosswalks, addresses and directional platforms. Regional station cards retain the coarse “elevated or at grade” classification where no more detailed station-type source was imported.
- [Chicago Cityscape / Yonah Freemark entrance observations](https://github.com/ChicagoCityscape/gis-data): CC0 historical coordinates for 123 downtown entrances. These are **not verified current entrance locations or availability**. The original dataset includes approximate/proposed-era observations. All points retain their geographic coordinates, source and historical-confidence label. Stair/entrance appearance is representative; some entries are signs at doors within existing buildings.
- [CTA Washington/Wabash opening](https://www.transitchicago.com/mayor-emanuel-opens-new-cta-train-station-at-washington-and-wabash/) and [Quincy renovation](https://www.transitchicago.com/cta-to-renovate-historic-quincy-loop-station/) inform the wave canopy and historic stationhouses. [Morgan](https://www.transitchicago.com/station/morg/) informs the glazed crossover/access structures; [Roosevelt](https://www.transitchicago.com/station/roos/) confirms its elevated/subway transfer complex. Geometry is a simplified exterior interpretation, not a measured architectural model.

**Operating overrides.** [State/Lake elevated](https://www.transitchicago.com/brownline/) is closed into 2029; retain its location with closure information and no train stops. Lake Red subway remains separate and open. [Lake Street Bridge rail service resumed October 29](https://www.transitchicago.com/lake-street-bridge-west-leaf-reconstruction-complete-cta-green-and-pink-line-service-to-return-to-normal-thursday-morning-/); the lower roadway stays closed. Traffic routing excludes that road span. [Red Line Extension construction](https://www.transitchicago.com/rle/construction/) does not create active stops: Red ends at 95th/Dan Ryan. Purple downtown service is explicitly an illustrative weekday rush-period scenario. Other operators, live arrivals, underground trains/interiors and passenger boarding are excluded.

**Physical corrections and uncertainty.** `tools/transit-corrections.json` lists 17 individual source-footprint replacements with reasons; no blanket corridor deletion is used. They replace solid station/rail-side roof extrusions with open elevated structures. The imported GIS lacks the north-turn connector at Tower 12; dated GTFS supplies that connection. A separately recorded lateral profile through the South Side alley keeps train bodies clear of retained roof envelopes. The [City's 1136–1140 Wabash development/right-of-way document](https://occprodstoragev1.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/lsmatterattachmentspublic/071d2ecf-1327-4fa5-89cc-620a9d394df9.pdf) supports the rail corridor beside the parcels; the fitted positions are estimates, with roughly six metres maximum correction. Original regional shapes are preserved. This is a documented review exception, not a claim of five-metre absolute accuracy.

Rail profiles are estimated at 8.9 m on ordinary segments, rising to approximately 11.4 m over Wells/Lake bridges. Track center offsets are generally 1.8 m, widening around Roosevelt's estimated 6.4 m island platform. Roosevelt's 122 m platform model is centered at local (101.1, 1628); the original station/platform reference coordinates remain in the asset. Other platform lengths are representative 126–130 m dimensions. Columns, trusses, landings, canopy dimensions and entrance orientations still need measured plans or dated imagery before they can be called verified. These limitations are also exposed in the public transit dialog.

The animation uses independent car positions, acceleration/braking, 12-second dwell and junction/curve reservations. Its extra clearance blocks, including a conservative shared north approach and combined Tower 12/Balbo block, are simulation safeguards, **not a reproduction of CTA signaling or capacity**. Brown goes counterclockwise; Orange, Pink and Purple Express clockwise; Green passes through via Lake/Wabash. Cars are representative 14.6 m bodies with 14.9 m spacing: Brown/Orange eight, Purple/Green six, Pink four. Colored stripes indicate service for this visualization and do not claim a dated fleet/livery assignment.

It checks termini, ordered Loop patterns, closures, station identity/projection, source/correction hashes, train fit, geographic clipping, retained-building clearance, pause/resume, and a two-hour motion audit with 28,800 car-volume collision samples. The review page adds fixed views for Morgan, both bridges, Mart, Clark/Lake, Washington/Wabash, Quincy, Roosevelt, subway entrances and the Loop, plus a CTA scene/road-closure/camera check. Passing these checks does not replace the unresolved entrance, elevation and architectural surveys described above.
